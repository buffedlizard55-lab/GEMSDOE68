Each candidate names the layers involved, the physical signature targeted, why it could catch a fault the USGS / INGENIOUS catalogue misses rather than one already in it, and how it differs from what this repository already implements. H1 — auto-context stacked refinement implemented, this session

Layers
the 19 DrivenData feature bands, 6 derived gradient channels, 11 lidar scarp channels, 8 GeoDAWN radiometric / magnetic channels, and the visible-fault catalogue
Physical signature
the stage-1 probability field sampled along and across the local structure-tensor orientation, and its multi-scale anisotropic smooths — i.e. continuity, parallelism and en échelon spacing of the geophysical lineament
Why off-catalogue
a per-pixel model fires on isolated anomalies; the stacked model fires where an anomaly continues past the end of a mapped trace, which is exactly where an unmapped splay or a stepover strand lives
Difference from the repo
every prior member of this family emits dots from a geometric detector (ridge / scarp / worm / euler lineament). This is the first supervised stacked model in the registry: the context features are learned, not hand-tuned
Cost
low — the channel stack was already cached; ~4 min per holdout fold on 2 CPU cores

H2 — damage-zone width asymmetry from lidar scarp polarity

Layers
downface_max, upface_max, step_max (2 m lidar aggregated to 100 m), det_elev, det_elev_slope
Physical signature
the across-strike asymmetry of the scarp: a normal fault drops the hanging wall, so the steep face and the fractured damage zone sit on opposite sides of the trace. An erosional escarpment has no such asymmetry
Why off-catalogue
the catalogue maps the trace, not the damage zone; where the trace is unmapped but the damage zone is expressed in the lidar, the asymmetry still points at the fault
Difference from the repo
prior lidar work used scarp magnitude; this uses scarp polarity and asymmetry, which is a signed quantity the magnitude throws away
Cost
low; the channels are already in the cached stack

H3 — echelon spacing as a spacing prior, not a line detector

Layers
tmi_hg, tc, mag_gx/gy, iso_grav_anom_hg, plus the mapped catalogue
Physical signature
the regular spacing of en échelon Riedel shears in a releasing bend: a quasi-periodic pattern in the magnetic horizontal gradient whose wavelength is set by the brittle layer thickness, not by the fault length
Why off-catalogue
individual short Riedel shears are below the mapping resolution of the published catalogue, but their spacing is a regional quantity that survives aggregation
Difference from the repo
no prior experiment used a periodicity / spacing statistic; all prior work was edge or ridge detection
Cost
medium; needs an autocorrelation or FFT feature along the local orientation

H4 — geothermal alteration halo in the radiometric ratios

Layers
GeoDAWN K, Th, U, TC and the contractor ratios ThK, UK, UTh (doi:10.5066/P93LGLVQ)
Physical signature
potassic and argillic alteration haloes around a geothermal system raise K and depress Th and U, so the ratios move even where the absolute counts are dominated by lithology
Why off-catalogue
the catalogue maps faults, not alteration; a fault that carries a hydrothermal halo but no topographic scarp is invisible to every scarp-based detector in this family and visible here
Difference from the repo
the ratios are already in the cached stack but have never been used as a *habitat* prior; they enter here as an explicit alteration-halo channel
Cost
low, but the interpretation is geological rather than geomorphological and needs the alteration literature checked before it is trusted

H5 — seismicity depth clustering as a 3-D constraint

Layers
deq_n100a15, ieq_n100a15 (earthquake distance / intensity), depth_to_base_surf, cond_surf
Physical signature
a fault that is actively slipping produces a depth-clustered seismicity cloud whose plan-view centroid is offset from the mapped trace by the dip of the fault; the offset direction and magnitude carry the dip
Why off-catalogue
the catalogue is a map of the surface trace; a dipping fault whose surface expression is hidden by alluvium still has a seismicity centroid that can be projected back to the surface along the dip
Difference from the repo
no prior experiment used the seismicity channels as a 3-D constraint rather than as a 2-D proximity feature
Cost
high, and it needs hypocentre depths that the bundled deq_n100a15 band does not carry
Data needed
USGS ANSS Comprehensive Catalog (via the USGS Earthquake Catalog API, earthquake.usgs.gov/fdsnws/event/1) for hypocentre depths in the study window. Not obtainable from this sandbox (the host is outside the allowed network list), so H5 is recorded as not viable in this session rather than proposed.
