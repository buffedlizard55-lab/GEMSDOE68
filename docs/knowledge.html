1. The mechanism (why a pruned dot file beats richer geology)

[official] DTI(α=0.2, β=0.8) with triangular kernel k(d)=max(1−d/300 m, 0):

TP_w = Σ_g max_x p(x)k(d), FP_w = Σ_x p(x)[1−max_g k(d)], FN_w = |G|−TP_w

(problem description, fetched 2026-10-20; worked example 3.00/1.89/2.00 → 0.60).

With T=TP_w, S=Σp (emitted mass), M=Σ p·max_g k, algebra gives

DTI = T / (0.2·(T+S−M) + 0.8·|G|), and **one more unit of mass raises DTI iff its kernel credit

exceeds 0.2·DTI** (≈0.056 at DTI 0.28) — see 01 for the derivation. Three consequences, each

visible in the family ladder [owner-report]: h19-5 solid 121,131 px → 0.1922; dotted d1.5

60,069 px → 0.2477; dotted d2.8 44,090 px → 0.2600; minus d(catalogue)=1 px dots → 0.2708;

minus d=2 px dots → 0.2778.

  • Mass discipline ≈ +0.07 on the same field. Thinning deletes pixels whose realised credit

    • is below the bar. This is pure decision theory, not geology, and it is where wide ribbons,

    continuous probability surfaces and most geometric-detector lanes (0.03–0.15) lose.

  • The catalogue halo is FP tax. The organiser's truth is new faults and known faults are

    • masked from credit; dots within 100–200 m of a mapped trace mostly earn 0 while paying 0.2.

    Both halo deletions gained. Any model that keys on proximity to the published catalogue fights

    this lesson — the direct motivation for this session's AC68-N1 ablation.

  • At fixed pixel set, 1.0 is optimal (scaling down scales T and (S−M) together), hence binary

    • dots, and hence the whole game after pruning is ranking: which ≈38 k pixels carry the most

    kernel credit toward unmapped faults.

Interpretation: the champion's average dot sits at a kernel credit of roughly 0.12 (i.e. typically

within ~150–250 m of some new-fault pixel, or covering one partially). Nobody reaches 0.32–0.41

by emission thrift — the pruning ladder already harvested that (+0.004–0.007 per late rung). The

leaders' top ≈38 k pixels must simply be nearer real unmapped faults, by 15–50 % in mean

credit. That is a detector/ranking problem, and it caps what any re-emission of an existing field

can do. It also says what a holdout must demonstrate to be live-relevant: a candidate field whose

top-ranked pixels recover withheld faults better at matched mass, especially away from the

catalogue — precisely this lane's gate.

Why auto-context is still the right lever (and its measured size): faults are extended objects —

continuous, parallel in NNE Basin-and-Range sets, en échelon at 1–3 km step-overs — so a weak

pixel on strike between two strong responses is more likely fault than an equal isolated pixel,

information no per-pixel model has. In this repo's whole-component holdout the stacked stages

beat stage 1 by +0.035…+0.055 HOLDOUT-DTI at 25–38 k dots with bootstrap CIs excluding 0

(evidence/exp2…, HOLDOUT-DTI, 11,321 withheld positives) — a relative gain over a per-pixel

LightGBM that is itself weaker than the h19-5 field. The honest reading: stacking is worth

roughly the right order of magnitude (+30–60 % relative on a per-pixel base), but only if the

base field is competitive and the gain survives >1 km from mapped faults, which is exactly where

exp1/exp2 are weakest and what AC68-N1 tests.

3. The contrarian point the leaderboard hides: the Final Prize Round

[official] (problem description, fetched today): the Initial Prize Round scores a fixed

private set of expert-mapped new faults (top 5 → $10 k each). The Final Prize Round ($250 k)

re-scores the same single chosen submission against an expanded label set that adds faults

experts verify after reviewing all teams' predictions: *"Predictions that helped experts identify

previously-unmapped faults can score higher here."*

Maximise P(Win) therefore does not mean maximising expected public-leaderboard DTI alone. A

submission that is a slight re-pruning of the incumbent field has almost no discovery upside; a

submission whose dots sit on genuinely unmapped but geologically defensible strands — far from

the catalogue, exactly where our >1 km gate applies — buys option value in the round worth 5× the

money, at some expected cost in round 1. The portfolio-correct target for this lane is: matched-

mass ranking at least as good as the incumbent family on recoverable faults, with as much of its

mass as possible on defensible off-catalogue strands. That is also why copying or lightly

perturbing the champion (prohibited here, and detectable) is strategically poor even before it is

a rules problem.
