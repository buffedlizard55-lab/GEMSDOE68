# Preregistered lane-68 hypotheses (2026-10-20; before the new fit)

**Decision rule (not an observed result):** keep the method in the auto-context lane. Compare a stage-2 LightGBM with stage 1 at identical emission budgets on outer spatial blocks, withholding *whole* fault segments with a buffer; require mean paired improvement > 2 SE both overall and > 1 km from any visible mapped fault. Canary each individual input on withheld truth, require AUC <= 0.90. Require pre-placement surface and final-dot checks against the available prior-raster registry; an overlap > 70% with *any* raster is a stop even when its dot density is high. No DrivenData submission slot is used. Every DTI is a local catalogue hide-and-recover proxy, not a public/private score.

**Why fault detection, not vent detection?** DrivenData's [official problem description](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/) states the target is faults indicative of geothermal resources. Predicting geothermal vents, springs or lithologic contacts *as labels* would miss the target. The [USGS GeoDAWN release](https://www.usgs.gov/data/geodawn-airborne-magnetic-and-radiometric-surveys-northwestern-great-basin-nevada-and) says magnetics constrain subsurface structure whereas radiometrics constrain surface geology/soil composition. The [Tu & Bai auto-context paper](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~ztu/publication/pami_autocontext.pdf) motivates learning from out-of-fold classifier maps together with the original image features; it does not establish geothermal performance.
