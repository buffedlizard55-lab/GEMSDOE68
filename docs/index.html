One-paragraph answer: why 0.2778, and can it be beaten?

The 0.2778 champion (h33-h33-2-b2, GEMSDOE32, owner-reported) is a binary field of 37,654 dots, the last rung of a pruning ladder on one per-pixel field (0.1922 solid → 0.2477 → 0.2600 → 0.2708 → 0.2778). The metric DTI = T/(0.2·(T+S−M)+0.8·|G|) charges 0.2 per emitted unit that is not within 300 m of an uncovered new fault, so deleting low-credit mass — first the field's tail, then the 100–200 m halo of the published catalogue — paid at every step. What is left to win is ranking: the same ≈38 k dots must carry more kernel credit toward real unmapped faults. That is a field problem, and auto-context (continuity, parallelism, en-échelon spacing learned from the stage-1 map) is the cheapest principled attack on it. The full derivation and provenance tags are in Why 0.2778; the measured result of this lane is on the Experiments page.
